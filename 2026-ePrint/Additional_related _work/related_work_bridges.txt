\f7\b0 \

\f8\i \cf0 \kerning1\expnd0\expndtw0 {\listtext	\uc0\u8226 	}\expnd0\expndtw0\kerning0

\f8\i \kerning1\expnd0\expndtw0 {\listtext	\uc0\u8226 	}\expnd0\expndtw0\kerning0

\ls4\ilvl0
\f8\i \kerning1\expnd0\expndtw0 {\listtext	\uc0\u8226 	}\expnd0\expndtw0\kerning0
Relevance:
\f7\i0  This work does 
\f6\b not
\f7\b0  address cross-chain bridges or light clients. It is strictly scoped to an internal consensus-layer problem, specifically mitigating vote-withholding among validators by tracking a two-dimensional "who observed whom" attestation matrix. Furthermore, the protocol does not utilize standard BLS signatures; it introduces a custom two-layer, pairing-based signature scheme grounded in the Random Modular Subset Sum (RMSS) assumption. 
\f6\b The sole intersection with our work is at the signature aggregation boundary.
\f7\b0  Both protocols confront the bottleneck of compressing massive signer subsets for commit-and-prove (CP) verification. However, their approaches diverge fundamentally: Fuchsbauer et al. invent a new algebraic signature primitive to allow a downstream, generic CP-SNARK to verify the matrix in poly-logarithmic time. In contrast, our Committee Key Scheme (CKS) retains standard cryptographic commitments for the validator vector and instead modifies the zero-knowledge architecture itself, utilizing a custom, monolithic hybrid-SNARK to achieve true $O(1)$ constant-time verification suitable for on-chain smart contracts.\
